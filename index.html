African-led capability • Regional collaboration • Defensible evidence

Building Africa's digital forensic capability — together.

WADFIN connects digital forensic practitioners, investigators, researchers, justice-sector professionals and institutional partners to strengthen evidence practice, professional visibility, African-led research and cross-border cooperation.

Why WADFIN?

Africa has capable practitioners. The challenge is that expertise, training, research, tools and professional networks remain unevenly distributed and insufficiently connected across borders.

Our proposition: move from avoidable dependency to stronger local capability — while remaining internationally connected.
95%of surveyed African member countries reported inadequate training, resource constraints or lack of specialised tools.
29%reported having a digital-evidence repository.
86%said international cooperation capacity needs improvement.
2 in 3said cyber-related offences represent a medium-to-high share of crime.
The capability ecosystem gap

The challenge is not a lack of African talent. It is connection, visibility and scale.

Digital evidence now sits across phones, cloud platforms, banks, telecoms, social media, connected devices and foreign jurisdictions. Yet practitioners often work without easy access to specialist peers, shared validation resources, visible research pathways or dependable regional professional networks.

01

Fragmented expertise

Strong practitioners may have limited access to peers who can help with unfamiliar devices, artefacts, platforms or specialised cases.

02

Limited professional visibility

African investigators and researchers need stronger platforms to publish, teach, mentor, collaborate and shape international discussions.

03

Uneven access to capability

Specialist tools, training, validation and forensic infrastructure can be expensive, fragmented or concentrated in a small number of institutions.

04

Cross-border friction

Digital evidence and suspects routinely cross jurisdictions, while operational relationships and lawful evidence pathways can be slow or difficult to navigate.

05

Inconsistent practice

Differences in acquisition, preservation, documentation and reporting can weaken confidence in otherwise valuable evidence.

06

Under-connected research

Practitioner knowledge, university research and operational experience are not always connected into a visible African-led evidence base.

Our strategic direction

From dependency to capability

Digital forensic sovereignty does not mean isolation. It means having enough local capability to make informed decisions, understand and validate tools, preserve evidence correctly and participate internationally from a position of competence.

Today / risk

  • External expertise required for many specialist cases
  • Fragmented and expensive training pathways
  • Limited visibility of regional specialists
  • Knowledge concentrated in individual institutions
  • Weak research-to-practice connections
→

WADFIN direction

  • Trusted regional practitioner network
  • Practitioner-led learning and mentorship
  • Open validation and evidence resources
  • African-led research and technical working groups
  • Stronger lawful cross-border professional connections

Founding story

A network born from a simple observation: capable people need to be able to find one another.

WADFIN was initiated by Sheikh Omar Jallow after recognising a recurring challenge across African digital forensics: talented investigators and researchers exist, but many remain professionally invisible to one another, separated by institutions, borders, uneven access to specialist resources and limited shared platforms.

The idea is therefore not to create another organisation that competes with existing agencies. It is to build a trusted professional layer that helps African expertise become more connected, visible, reusable and influential.

That same practical philosophy informs Sheikh's open-source forensic work, including ChronoTrace, a Chromium browser examination project, and TraceFrame, an open-source forensic initiative.
