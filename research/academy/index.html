WADFIN Academy

Practice-led learning for the digital evidence profession.

The Academy is WADFIN's professional learning arm. Its proposed programmes combine technical examination, investigative reasoning, evidence integrity, reporting, legal awareness and professional ethics for West African contexts.

Explore programmesTraining partnership enquiry
Learning framework

Three learning pathways

Programmes are designed as progressive professional development. Delivery may include instructor-led workshops, practical labs, case simulations, guided exercises, assessment and train-the-trainer activity.

FOUNDATION

Digital Evidence Foundations

For investigators, first responders, justice professionals and early-career practitioners who need reliable evidence-handling skills.

Typical duration: 2 to 5 days
PRACTITIONER

Forensic Examination & Investigation

For practitioners conducting computer, mobile, browser, OSINT and cyber-enabled crime examinations.

Typical duration: 3 to 10 days
ADVANCED

Quality, Leadership & Courtroom Practice

For senior examiners, team leads, trainers, laboratory managers and professionals responsible for quality and testimony.

Typical duration: 2 to 5 days

Core programmes

WDA 101

Digital Evidence First Responder

Foundation | 3 days | Practical

Introduces lawful identification, preservation, seizure, packaging, documentation and transfer of digital evidence.

Key modules

  • Digital evidence principles and scene awareness
  • Device identification and initial risk assessment
  • Preservation, isolation and contamination control
  • Chain of custody and contemporaneous notes
  • Packaging, transport and laboratory submission

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to recognise digital evidence, take proportionate preservation steps, document actions clearly and maintain evidential continuity.

Target participants: police officers, investigators, border and customs personnel, prosecutors, regulators and incident responders.
WDA 201

Computer Forensics Practitioner

Practitioner | 5 days | Lab-based

Develops a structured workflow for forensic acquisition, file-system examination, artefact interpretation and defensible reporting.

Key modules

  • Forensic acquisition and verification
  • File systems and operating-system artefacts
  • Deleted data and user activity reconstruction
  • Timeline analysis and corroboration
  • Examination notes and technical reporting

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to plan and conduct a documented computer examination, interpret common artefacts and communicate findings with appropriate limitations.

Target participants: digital forensic examiners, cybercrime investigators and technical investigators.
WDA 202

Mobile Device Forensics

Practitioner | 5 days | Lab-based

Covers acquisition choices, mobile artefacts, application data, timestamps, communications and structured interpretation.

Key modules

  • Mobile platforms and acquisition approaches
  • Logical, file-system and physical acquisition concepts
  • Application, communication and location artefacts
  • Timestamp interpretation and validation
  • Reporting and evidential limitations

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to select appropriate examination approaches, analyse common mobile artefacts and explain the reliability and limitations of findings.

Target participants: forensic practitioners and investigators with foundational digital evidence knowledge.
WDA 203

Browser & Internet Artefact Forensics

Practitioner | 3 days | Lab-based

Focuses on browser history, downloads, searches, cookies, login metadata, extensions and timeline reconstruction.

Key modules

  • Chromium and browser artefact structures
  • History, downloads and search activity
  • Cookies, autofill and login metadata
  • Extensions, sessions and supporting artefacts
  • Timeline reconstruction and validation

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to identify and interpret browser artefacts, correlate activity across sources and produce a transparent examination timeline.

Target participants: digital forensic practitioners, cybercrime investigators and analysts.
WDA 204

OSINT for Lawful Investigations

Practitioner | 3 days | Scenario-based

Builds disciplined open-source research skills with emphasis on verification, provenance, documentation, privacy and evidential value.

Key modules

  • Investigation planning and collection discipline
  • Search, social and web research techniques
  • Source verification and corroboration
  • Capture, preservation and provenance
  • Ethics, privacy and reporting

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to conduct documented open-source enquiries, evaluate source reliability and preserve relevant material responsibly.

Target participants: investigators, analysts, researchers and authorised public-sector teams.
WDA 301

Digital Forensic Reporting & Courtroom Practice

Advanced | 3 days | Workshop

Develops clear forensic reports and confident, impartial communication of technical findings to non-technical decision makers.

Key modules

  • Fact, interpretation and opinion
  • Report structure and exhibit referencing
  • Limitations, uncertainty and validation
  • Preparing for testimony
  • Mock examination and cross-examination

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to produce clear reports, explain methodology and limitations, and communicate technical evidence impartially.

Target participants: experienced examiners, investigators and technical witnesses.
WDA 302

Forensic Quality, Validation & Laboratory Practice

Advanced | 3 days | Workshop

Introduces repeatability, verification, tool testing, peer review, quality records and risk-based laboratory practice.

Key modules

  • Quality principles for digital forensics
  • Tool testing and method validation
  • Peer review and technical verification
  • Competence records and controlled procedures
  • Continuous improvement and corrective action

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to design practical quality controls, document validation activity and strengthen reproducibility within their teams.

Target participants: senior examiners, laboratory leads, supervisors and quality personnel.
WDA 401

Train the Digital Forensics Trainer

Faculty development | 5 days | Assessed

Supports sustainable local training capability by developing instructors who can plan, deliver and evaluate practical forensic learning.

Key modules

  • Learning objectives and lesson design
  • Practical lab and scenario development
  • Facilitation and learner support
  • Assessment and feedback
  • Instructor demonstration and peer review

Learning outcomes

Participants should be able to design and facilitate structured training, assess learning consistently and maintain course materials responsibly.

Target participants: experienced practitioners nominated for training or capacity-building roles.
Assessment

Learning that demonstrates competence

Depending on the programme, assessment may combine practical exercises, short knowledge checks, scenario work, examination notes, report writing and instructor observation. Certificates should state what was completed and assessed. WADFIN will not describe a course as externally accredited unless formal accreditation has been obtained.

Programme design principles

  • Practice-led and scenario-based
  • Evidence integrity before tool operation
  • Vendor-neutral concepts where practical
  • Transparent limitations and validation
  • Ethical and lawful professional practice
  • Adaptable to institutional and national contexts

Institutional training options

Agency Cohorts

Structured delivery for law enforcement, justice, regulatory and public-sector teams, adapted to agreed learning needs.

University Collaboration

Guest teaching, practical labs, curriculum dialogue, research supervision support and practitioner-academic exchange.

Train-the-Trainer

Capacity-building programmes that help institutions develop sustainable internal instructors and repeatable training materials.

Development status: WADFIN Academy is a developing programme framework. Course dates, faculty, fees, certification arrangements and institutional partnerships will be published only when confirmed.
