About WADFIN

Connecting expertise. Building capability. Strengthening African professional visibility.

WADFIN is being developed as a professional West African network for digital forensics, investigations, research and justice-sector cooperation.

Our purpose

Why WADFIN exists

Across West Africa, capable investigators, analysts, researchers, prosecutors and educators are already doing important work. The problem is that professional expertise is often difficult to discover, insufficiently connected across borders and not always supported by shared research, validation resources, mentorship or visible professional pathways.

WADFIN exists to help turn scattered capability into a connected professional ecosystem.

Core proposition

Africa should be able to investigate digital crime, preserve digital evidence and support justice with strong local capability — not as a substitute for international cooperation, but from a position of greater capacity, confidence and sovereignty.

Vision

A West Africa where digital evidence can be identified, preserved, analysed and presented with professional confidence, legal defensibility and strong African capability.

Mission

To connect practitioners, build capability, advance practical standards, promote African-led research and strengthen cooperation in digital forensics and investigations.

Strategic objectives

Build professional capability

Practical training, mentorship, peer learning and competency pathways.

Create a trusted network

Help specialist knowledge, lessons and lawful professional support move faster across borders.

Advance defensible practice

Promote stronger acquisition, preservation, analysis, documentation, reporting and quality assurance.

Strengthen legal readiness

Study regional legal frameworks and practical routes for lawful cross-border evidence work.

Promote African research

Create space for African practitioners to publish, present, mentor and shape technical discussions.

Improve resilience

Encourage tool diversity, open-source capability, validation and sustainable workflows.

Founding story

WADFIN was initiated by Sheikh Omar Jallow, a digital forensics professional who observed a persistent visibility and connection gap among African practitioners. Skilled professionals often operate within separate institutions and countries without a strong regional mechanism for discovering peers, exchanging non-sensitive knowledge, mentoring new investigators, validating tools or collectively contributing to research and professional standards.

The network is therefore designed around collaboration rather than duplication. WADFIN does not seek to replace national institutions, law-enforcement bodies, courts, prosecutors, regulators or international organisations.

Founding status: WADFIN is presently a founding concept under development. Governance, legal structure, membership rules and formal partnerships will be established through consultation and legal review.
